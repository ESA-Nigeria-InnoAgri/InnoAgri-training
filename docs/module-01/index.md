# Module 1 - Introduction to EO and Applications for Agriculture

> A **PDF version** of this week's program can be downloaded [here](../assets/schedules/InnoAgri_Week1_Participant_Programme_v2.pdf).


## Module overview

**Timing:** 16-20 November 2026  
**Level:** Basic  
**Lead:** VITO  
**Additional teachers:** RAMANI  
**Target audience:** Ministry of Agriculture, Ministry of Livestock, Ministry of Water Resources Management and relevant NASRDA staff

Module 1 provides a **high-level introduction to Earth Observation (EO) and its applications for agricultural monitoring**. It is designed to create a common understanding among participants with different technical and institutional backgrounds before the programme moves into more specialised modules.

The week also has an important co-development function. Alongside the introductory training, participants will discuss existing agricultural monitoring activities and datasets, assess their own technical capacity, and contribute to defining the **user needs, scope and priorities of the Climate-Smart Information System (CSIS)**.


## Learning objectives

By the end of the week, participants should be able to:

- explain the basic principles of Earth Observation and distinguish between optical, radar, thermal and LiDAR observations;
- describe key advantages and limitations of satellite, UAV and complementary data sources for agricultural monitoring;
- recognise a range of EO-based agricultural monitoring applications;
- understand how recent developments such as cloud computing and AI are changing the way EO data can be used;
- identify relevant existing agricultural monitoring systems, datasets and capabilities within their own institutions;
- retrieve and manipulate basic geospatial datasets as part of an initial practical capacity assessment;
- identify priority information needs and user groups for the CSIS; and
- contribute to the conceptual co-design of future CSIS services.


## Main topics

The week will cover the following broad themes:

1. **Introduction to Earth Observation**
    - Optical Earth Observation
    - Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
    - Thermal Earth Observation
    - LiDAR
    - Satellite versus UAV observations
    - Strengths, limitations and complementarity of different data sources

2. **EO applications for agriculture**
    - Crop monitoring
    - Crop type and cropland mapping
    - Crop condition and early warning
    - Crop suitability
    - Yield assessment
    - Field-scale monitoring and precision agriculture
    - Examples of operational agricultural information systems

3. **Recent developments in EO**
    - Cloud-based EO processing
    - Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning
    - New opportunities for scalable and operational agricultural monitoring

4. **Existing systems and institutional assets**
    - Examples of Climate-Smart Information Systems and related operational platforms
    - Existing NASRDA agricultural monitoring activities
    - Existing institutional datasets and services
    - Discussion of strengths, gaps and opportunities for reuse

5. **CSIS prototypes and expectations**
    - Overview of proposed CSIS services
    - State-of-the-art approaches and basic requirements
    - Discussion of realistic expectations and implementation priorities

6. **Data and capacity assessment**
    - Inventory of available EO, meteorological, survey and in-situ datasets
    - Initial practical geospatial assignment
    - Identification of current technical capacities and gaps

7. **CSIS needs assessment and co-creation**
    - Target users
    - Priority decisions and information needs
    - Thematic scope
    - Preferred services and outputs
    - Group discussion and consolidation of user requirements


## Prerequisites

Module 1 is an introductory course and does **not require advanced programming experience**.

All participants are requested to:
    - Bring a laptop with QGIS installed.
    - Register for a free account on the [Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/).
    - Think of practical agricultural monitoring questions and decisions relevant to your work.
    - Bring examples of datasets, reports, monitoring tools or information services used by your organisation, if available and shareable.
    - Bring details of colleagues who can answer follow-up questions about data availability and use.
